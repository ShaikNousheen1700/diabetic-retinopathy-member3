About diabetic retinopathy

What is diabetic retinopathy?

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is an eye condition that can develop in people with diabetes. Over time, high blood sugar can damage the tiny blood vessels of the retina. Early stages often cause no noticeable symptoms, which is why regular eye examinations matter.

Why the retina matters

The retina is the thin, light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. Like the sensor in a camera, it turns light into signals that travel through the optic nerve to the brain. Its centre, the macula, gives us sharp central vision for reading and recognising faces.

How blood vessels are affected

Damaged retinal vessels can weaken, bulge, leak fluid or blood, or become blocked. When parts of the retina lose their blood supply, the eye may grow new, fragile vessels that bleed easily.

Why early detection helps

Diabetic retinopathy is one of the leading causes of vision loss in working-age adults. Because it can progress silently, finding changes early gives doctors the chance to monitor and treat it before vision is affected.

How it works

From a retinal photograph to a severity grade

Five steps happen every time you analyse an image.

  1. 1UploadA retinal fundus image (PNG or JPEG).
  2. 2PreprocessCrop the dark border and resize to 224 × 224 pixels.
  3. 3Extract featuresDenseNet121 detects patterns such as vessels, spots and lesions.
  4. 4ClassifyA final layer scores each of the five grades.
  5. 5ResultThe most likely grade with its confidence and all five probabilities.

What is DenseNet121?

DenseNet121 is a convolutional neural network with 121 layers, designed to learn visual patterns from images. Its special idea is dense connections: inside each block, every layer receives the outputs of all earlier layers, not only the one before it.

This lets the network reuse features. Fine details found early — like the tiny dots of an early lesion — stay available to later layers, while the model stays compact (about 7 million parameters).

We used transfer learning: the network started from weights learned on millions of everyday images (ImageNet) and was then fine-tuned on retinal images.

  1. Retinal fundus imageAny size · PNG / JPEG
  2. Image preprocessingBorder crop · resize · normalisation224 × 224 × 3
  3. DenseNet1214 dense blocks · ImageNet pre-trained
  4. Feature extractionGlobal average pooling7 × 7 × 1024 → 1024
  5. 5-class classificationDense layer with softmax5 probabilities
  6. Prediction + confidenceHighest probability = predicted grade

APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection

A public dataset of retinal fundus photographs collected in India and released on Kaggle. Each labelled image carries one of the five severity grades. We used the 3,662 labelled training images, split with a fixed seed and the same class proportions in every part:

About the project

Comparative Analysis of Deep Learning Models for Diabetic Retinopathy Severity Classification

The project studies how well different convolutional neural networks grade diabetic retinopathy from fundus images, when all of them are trained and tested under the same conditions.

Objective

Compare DenseNet, ResNet and EfficientNet for 5-class DR severity classification on APTOS 2019, and provide a working prediction system.

Common conditions

Same data split (70 / 15 / 15, seed 42), 224 × 224 input, ImageNet transfer learning, the same training schedule and the same evaluation metrics.

Technology

Python · TensorFlow / Keras · OpenCV · NumPy · Pandas · scikit-learn · Matplotlib · Seaborn · FastAPI · Google Colab (GPU training).
